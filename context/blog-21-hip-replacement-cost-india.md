## What's Included in the India Cost

This is the question that matters — because a low headline number that excludes half the actual costs isn't useful.

A standard hip replacement cost estimate from [Sano Healthcare and Tourism](https://www.sanohealth.co.in) includes:

- Surgeon's fee
- Anaesthesiologist's fee
- Hospital stay (typically 3–5 days)
- Operating theatre and equipment charges
- The implant itself
- Pre-operative investigations (blood work, X-rays, ECG)
- Standard post-operative medications during hospital stay
- Physiotherapy during the hospital stay

**What's typically separate:**
- Return flights and airport transfers
- Recovery accommodation outside the hospital
- Personal expenses during your stay

Even adding flights and two weeks of accommodation, the total cost for most patients remains 60–70% below what the surgery alone would cost at home.

## The Implant Question — Because Everyone Asks

The most common concern patients have: *"Is it the same implant I'd get at home, or a cheaper version?"*

At NABH-accredited hospitals in India, the answer is: the same brands.

Zimmer Biomet, Stryker, DePuy Synthes, and Smith & Nephew — the four major global orthopaedic implant manufacturers — all supply Indian hospitals. The ceramic, metal, or polyethylene components going into your hip are manufactured to the same specifications as those used in the UK or US.

The difference isn't the implant. It's the cost of the procedure around it — surgeon fees, hospital operating costs, and anaesthesia — which are structurally lower in India than in Western healthcare markets.

Manufacturer warranties on implants are international and are not voided by surgery outside the country of manufacture. If you need revision surgery at home years later, the implant records transfer with you.

## Implant Types Available in India

Your orthopaedic surgeon will guide the right choice based on your age, activity level, bone quality, and anatomy — but here's a brief orientation:

**Cemented implants** — Implant fixed with bone cement. The most commonly used approach, with 20+ year outcomes data. Suited to most patients over 60.

**Cementless implants** — Press-fitted and fixed through bone ingrowth over time. Preferred for younger, more active patients where long-term durability and bone preservation are priorities.

**Ceramic-on-ceramic** — Highly wear-resistant bearing surface. Preferred for younger patients who may put more long-term load on the joint.

**Metal-on-polyethylene** — Standard bearing surface combination, proven record, cost-effective.

**Resurfacing** — Bone-conserving alternative to full replacement, suitable for specific anatomy. Less commonly performed but available at specialist centres.

## The Waiting List Problem

In the UK's NHS, the median wait for elective hip replacement is currently over a year in many trusts. In Canada, patients in some provinces wait 12–20 months from specialist referral to surgery. In Australia, public hospital waits routinely exceed 12 months.

During that wait, the joint continues to deteriorate. Compensatory movement patterns — the unconscious adjustments your body makes to avoid pain — create secondary problems in the lower back, knee, and opposite hip. Patients who finally reach surgery after a year on a waiting list often arrive in worse overall condition than they would have been six months earlier.

In India, the same procedure can be scheduled within days of your inquiry. Zero waiting period isn't a marketing phrase — it's a structural feature of how private hospital systems in India operate.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is hip replacement in India safe?**
At NABH and JCI-accredited hospitals, yes. Accreditation is independently audited and covers infection control, surgical safety, anaesthesia protocols, and clinical outcomes. All hospitals in [Sano's network](https://www.sanohealth.co.in/app/pages/treatments.html) are accredited. [Read about doctor quality in India →](https://www.sanohealth.co.in/app/pages/news.html)

**Will my home insurance cover hip replacement in India?**
Some international health insurance policies cover planned treatment abroad — check for "overseas elective treatment" clauses. JCI-accredited hospitals are more commonly recognised by international insurers. Travel insurance covers unrelated emergencies during your stay, not the planned procedure itself.

**What if I need revision surgery after I return home?**
Full implant documentation — model numbers, surgical notes, imaging — is provided before you leave India and transferable to any orthopaedic surgeon at home. Revision surgery can be performed domestically with this record.

**Can both hips be replaced at once in India?**
Yes — bilateral hip replacement is available. Many Western systems decline to do both simultaneously. In India, where surgical teams perform high volumes, it's a standard offering where the patient's health allows it. [Compare bilateral hip replacement costs →](https://www.sanohealth.co.in/#compare)

**How long until I can walk normally after hip replacement?**
Most patients walk with support within 24 hours of surgery. A cane or walker is used for 2–4 weeks. Most daily activities resume by 6–8 weeks. Full recovery — including confident, unrestricted movement — typically takes 3–6 months.
