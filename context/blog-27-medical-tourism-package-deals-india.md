# What's Included in Medical Tourism Package Deals from Indian Hospitals?

"Package deal" means different things depending on who's offering it.

Some Indian hospitals bundle surgery, stay, meals, and aftercare into a single number. Others quote a surgery fee and leave everything else to you. And some quotes sit in between — partial bundles that look comprehensive until you read the itemised breakdown.

Knowing what to look for before you commit saves both money and surprise.

---

## What a Good Package Should Include

At NABH-accredited hospitals working with international patients, a complete package typically covers:

**The clinical essentials:**
- Surgeon's fee
- Anaesthesiologist's fee
- Operating theatre charges
- Hospital stay (number of nights specified)
- Standard nursing care during admission
- The implant or medical device (for surgical cases)
- Pre-operative investigations — blood work, ECG, chest X-ray
- Post-operative medications during the hospital stay
- In-hospital physiotherapy (for orthopaedic and cardiac cases)

**International patient services:**
- Dedicated international patient coordinator during your stay
- Translation and interpreter support — particularly relevant if you're travelling with [Sano Healthcare and Tourism](https://www.sanohealth.co.in), whose Ewan-powered multilingual team covers 120+ languages throughout the journey
- Assistance with discharge documentation and medical records

A well-structured package from a reputable hospital lists each of these line by line. If a quote arrives as a single number with no breakdown, ask for the itemisation before you read anything else.

## What's Usually Not Included

This is where patients get caught out. Standard packages at most hospitals do not cover:

**Travel and logistics:**
- Return flights
- Airport pickup and drop-off (though this is often arranged separately — [see how Sano handles airport transfers →](https://www.sanohealth.co.in/app/pages/blog-airport-pickup-accommodation-india.html))
- Accommodation outside the hospital stay

**Extended care:**
- Recovery accommodation after hospital discharge
- Meals during your post-discharge recovery stay
- Companion accommodation (your family member's hotel)

**Additional clinical needs:**
- Repeat investigations if your reports are outdated
- Medications prescribed at discharge to take home
- Post-operative physiotherapy after hospital discharge
- Any treatment needed for complications arising outside the standard episode

**Administrative:**
- Visa fees and documentation
- Travel insurance

None of this is hidden — it's standard structure across most private hospitals globally. The important thing is knowing what isn't in the number before you start comparing quotes.

## How to Read a Package Quote

When you receive a cost estimate, run through this checklist:

**Is the implant included?** For joint replacement, cardiac valve surgery, and similar procedures, the implant cost is often the single largest line item. Some quotes include it; some don't. Confirm explicitly.

**How many hospital nights are included?** A package that covers 3 nights may look cheaper than one covering 5 — but if your procedure typically requires 5 nights and you need 2 more, those are billed additionally.

**Are pre-operative investigations included?** Blood work, imaging, ECG before surgery. Sometimes included, sometimes billed as a separate block.

**What happens if you need to stay longer?** Ask the per-night rate for additional hospital days beyond what the package covers.

**What's the complication protocol?** If additional surgery or extended ICU care is needed, how is that billed? A good hospital answers this directly.

## All-Inclusive vs. Itemised — Which Is Better?

Some facilitators and hospitals offer fully all-inclusive packages — surgery, flights, accommodation, meals, transfers, all in one price. These can be convenient, but they're worth examining carefully:

**Convenience comes at a cost.** Bundled packages typically carry a margin above what you'd pay arranging each component separately. The convenience is real; so is the premium.

**Less flexibility.** Bundled accommodation and flights leave less room to extend your recovery stay, upgrade your room, or adjust your departure date if your surgeon recommends more time.

**Harder to compare.** An all-in package from one provider and an itemised quote from another are genuinely difficult to compare unless you unbundle both.

For most patients, a transparent itemised quote — where each component is priced separately — gives better value and more control. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I negotiate the package price?**
At many hospitals, yes — particularly if you're paying in cash or if your procedure is being booked through a facilitator with an existing hospital relationship. [Sano's team](https://www.sanohealth.co.in) negotiates on your behalf as part of the standard service.

**What if I need more nights than the package covers?**
Additional nights are billed at the hospital's per-night rate. Ask for this figure before you confirm any package — so you're not calculating it mid-recovery.

**Are meals included in the hospital stay?**
Standard hospital meals during admission are typically included. Specialist dietary requirements — halal, diabetic, low-sodium — should be confirmed with the international patient desk before arrival, not on the day.
