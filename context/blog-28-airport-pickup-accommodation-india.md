# How Do I Arrange Airport Pickup and Accommodation Near the Hospital in India?

Landing in an unfamiliar country at midnight after a long flight, not knowing who's meeting you or where you're sleeping — that's the version of this experience nobody wants.

It's also entirely avoidable. Here's how airport transfers and accommodation work for international patients in India, and what to have confirmed before you board.

---

## Airport Pickup — What to Have in Place Before You Land

At minimum, three things should be confirmed in writing before your flight:

**Who is meeting you.** A named person, not "our driver." You should have their full name, a photo if possible, and a WhatsApp number that works internationally. They should know your flight number and arrival terminal.

**What they're holding.** A sign with your name, in a language you'll recognise. Simple, but worth confirming.

**What happens if there's a delay.** Your flight lands 90 minutes late. Do they wait? Is there a number to call? Know this before you need it.

## Accommodation — What to Look For

Your accommodation during a medical trip is not a holiday choice. The criteria are different.

**Proximity to the hospital.** The most important factor. In the early days post-surgery, even a 20-minute journey feels long. Within 2–3km of your treating hospital is the target. In the event of a complication or a sudden follow-up need, distance matters.

**Accessibility.** Ground floor or lift access. Wide doorways if you'll be using a walker or wheelchair post-surgery. A wet room or walk-in shower rather than a bath. These aren't luxuries — they're functional requirements for recovering patients.

**Air conditioning.** Non-negotiable in most Indian cities, particularly outside the October–February window. Confirm it's in the room, not just the lobby.

**Food.** Either in-house dining that can accommodate your dietary requirements, or reliable delivery options. In the early days of recovery, you won't be going out to find meals. Your clinical nutrition plan — [coordinated by Sano's team](https://www.sanohealth.co.in) — needs to be workable from wherever you're staying.

**Quiet.** Indian cities are loud. A room away from the street, or a property in a quieter area near the hospital, makes a real difference to sleep quality during recovery.

**Reliable WiFi.** For remote follow-up consultations with your Indian doctor, video calls with family, and general peace of mind.

## Types of Accommodation That Work Well

**Hospital-affiliated guesthouses.** Many major hospitals run or partner with guesthouses within walking distance. Functional, medically aware, often cheaper than hotels. Not always the most comfortable, but hard to beat for proximity and convenience.

**Serviced apartments.** The best option for stays longer than a week or for patients with companions. Kitchen access means you can control your diet without relying on restaurant delivery for every meal. More space than a hotel room makes recovery significantly more comfortable.

**Medically familiar hotels.** Some hotels near major hospital clusters have experience with recovering patients — accessible rooms, flexible checkout, relationships with nearby pharmacies, and front desk staff who understand that a guest might need help at unusual hours. These are worth seeking out specifically.

Sano arranges accommodation based on your procedure, your mobility requirements, your dietary needs, and your budget — not a generic hotel booking. The right option for a patient recovering from cardiac surgery is different from the right option for someone doing a dental implant trip.

**Can I arrange my own airport pickup independently?**
Yes — ride-sharing apps (Uber and Ola both operate in India's major cities) work reliably at airports. For the cost involved, a pre-arranged pickup with a named coordinator is a better use of that energy.

**How far in advance should accommodation be booked?**
As soon as your surgical date is confirmed. Good accommodation near major hospital clusters fills quickly, particularly during October–February when India's medical tourism volumes are highest.
