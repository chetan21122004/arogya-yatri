# Will My Health Insurance Cover Surgery Done in India?

The short answer: sometimes. And the longer answer is worth reading before you assume either way.

Whether your insurance covers surgery in India depends on your specific policy, your insurer, your procedure, and — in some cases — which hospital you choose. Patients who check carefully often find more coverage than they expected. Those who assume they're covered without checking sometimes find out otherwise at the worst possible moment.

---

## The Three Situations Patients Are Usually In

**1. You have international health insurance**
Some policies — particularly those sold to expats, frequent travellers, or employees of multinational companies — include coverage for planned treatment anywhere in the world, including India. If your policy says "worldwide coverage" or "global health plan," read the fine print specifically for elective or planned treatment. Many of these policies cover it; some exclude it or require pre-authorisation.

**2. You have domestic health insurance**
Most domestic policies — NHS top-up plans, US employer plans, Australian private health insurance — only cover treatment within your home country or a defined network. Surgery in India typically falls outside this network and won't be reimbursed. There are exceptions, but they're uncommon.

**3. You're paying out of pocket**
This is the situation most medical tourists are actually in — and it's not a problem. The cost of surgery in India, even without insurance, is often lower than the out-of-pocket cost of the same procedure at a domestic private hospital. ## How to Check Your Policy — What to Actually Look For

Don't call your insurer and ask "does this cover India?" The answer will be vague. Ask these specific questions instead:

**"Does my policy cover planned or elective treatment outside my home country?"**
This is the core question. Planned surgery abroad is treated differently from emergency care abroad by almost every insurer.

**"If covered, does the hospital need to meet any specific accreditation standard?"**
Many insurers that do cover overseas treatment require the hospital to be JCI-accredited. This is relevant because not all NABH-accredited hospitals in India hold JCI accreditation — though the major ones do. All hospitals in [Sano's network](https://www.sanohealth.co.in/app/pages/treatments.html) are NABH-accredited; several also hold JCI.

**"Do I need pre-authorisation before the surgery takes place?"**
Most insurers require this for planned procedures. If pre-authorisation is needed and you don't get it before your surgery, the claim will likely be rejected regardless of whether the procedure would otherwise be covered.

**"What documentation will I need for the claim?"**
Get this list in writing before you travel. Indian hospitals at accreditation standard produce complete documentation — operative reports, itemised bills, pathology — but knowing exactly what your insurer needs lets you collect everything in one go rather than chasing it from abroad afterwards.

## JCI Accreditation — Why It Matters for Insurance

If your insurer does cover overseas treatment, JCI accreditation is the most commonly required hospital standard. Joint Commission International is the global benchmark used by insurers in the US, Europe, and the Middle East to verify that a hospital meets international safety and quality standards.

India has roughly 30 JCI-accredited hospitals — a small number relative to the total, but including several of the country's most established international patient centres. ## What Travel Insurance Covers — and What It Doesn't

Travel insurance is not the same as health insurance, and it doesn't cover your planned surgery. What it does cover is everything around it — emergency medical events unrelated to your procedure, trip cancellation, missed connections, lost luggage.

The key limitation: most travel insurance policies exclude pre-existing conditions. The condition you're travelling to treat is, by definition, pre-existing — so complications arising from your planned procedure are typically not covered by travel insurance either.

This is handled at the hospital level rather than the insurance level. NABH-accredited hospitals have defined protocols for managing surgical complications — additional care required within the same treatment episode is covered as part of your treatment agreement with the hospital, not claimed separately on insurance.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**My insurer said they cover "overseas emergency treatment" — does that include planned surgery?**
Almost certainly not. Emergency treatment abroad and planned elective surgery abroad are treated as separate categories by virtually every insurer. Overseas emergency coverage kicks in when something unexpected happens while you're travelling — not for a procedure you scheduled weeks in advance.

**Can I claim back Indian hospital costs after I return home?**
Only if your policy specifically covers planned treatment abroad and you followed the pre-authorisation process before the surgery. Attempting to claim retrospectively without pre-authorisation is usually rejected.

**Does it help to get a letter from my doctor at home recommending India?**
It can support a pre-authorisation request in some cases — particularly if you're arguing that the procedure isn't available or the waiting time is unreasonable at home. It doesn't override a policy exclusion.

**What documentation should I collect from the Indian hospital?**
At minimum: itemised invoice, official receipt, operative report, discharge summary, and any pathology or imaging reports. Ask your insurer for their specific requirements before you travel. [Sano's team](https://www.sanohealth.co.in) can help compile this documentation before you leave India.

**What if my insurer covers part of the cost?**
Partial reimbursement is better than none. Some insurers apply a daily hospital benefit or a fixed surgical benefit that covers a portion of the cost, even if they don't cover the full amount. Worth claiming whatever is available.
